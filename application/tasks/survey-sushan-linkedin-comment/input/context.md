# Scenario Context

## DBS Bank

DBS Group Holdings is a Singapore-headquartered banking and financial services institution with operations across Greater China, Southeast Asia, and South Asia. It is one of Singapore's three major local banks and has a particularly large domestic presence.

DBS also owns the POSB franchise. POSB has historically held a strong community identity in Singapore and has long been associated with everyday retail banking, including lower-income households and older generations. This gives DBS a relationship with the Singapore public that extends beyond that of a purely commercial institution.

## Leadership

Tan Su Shan became Chief Executive Officer of DBS in March 2025 after previously holding senior leadership positions across the bank's wealth management, consumer banking, and institutional banking businesses.

Her stated strategic direction includes the idea of building an **"AI-enabled bank with a heart"**, combining continued technology adoption with an emphasis on human connection and customer experience.

## Digital Banking and Operational Context

DBS has invested heavily in digital transformation and has sought to operate with the technological capabilities of a technology company rather than a traditional bank. Its operating model includes large-scale digital platforms, cloud-oriented architecture, artificial intelligence, and machine-learning systems.

At the same time, the bank has faced public scrutiny over the reliability of its digital services.

Between 2021 and 2025, DBS experienced several high-profile digital disruptions. A major incident in October 2023 led to regulatory intervention by the Monetary Authority of Singapore and increased scrutiny of the bank's operational resilience.

On 19 March 2026, DBS experienced another digital banking disruption affecting its mobile applications, online banking services, DBS PayLah!, and PayNow access. Public reports of problems peaked at more than 2,100 during the disruption. DBS stated that customer funds remained safe, and services were restored after approximately one hour. MAS subsequently said it was monitoring the incident and the bank's investigation into its cause.

As a result, DBS's public identity contains a tension between its reputation as a large, technologically advanced and financially established institution and continuing public expectations that its everyday digital banking services should operate reliably.

Its long-standing community presence through DBS and POSB can contribute to strong institutional trust, while service disruptions can also attract significant criticism because customers depend heavily on the bank for everyday payments and banking activities.

## Situation at the End of the Thread

The publicly visible sequence therefore consists of:

1. A customer reporting that a banking service had been unavailable to them for more than 15 days and that an earlier customer-care complaint had not resolved the issue.
2. The customer escalating the matter publicly on LinkedIn and tagging senior DBS leaders.
3. DBS CEO Tan Su Shan personally acknowledging the complaint approximately one hour later and stating that the technology team had checked the issue and that someone would respond.
