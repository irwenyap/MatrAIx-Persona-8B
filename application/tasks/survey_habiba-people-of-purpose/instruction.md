# Habiba People of Purpose Social Media Reaction

You have encountered a DBS/POSB social media post featuring **Habiba Begum Hajamohidin**, a Collections Officer, as part of the **Humans of POSB** storytelling series.

The post is intended for DBS's Facebook and Instagram audiences.

Read the supplied scenario context carefully before answering the questionnaire.

## How to respond

Answer from your own perspective and behave as you realistically would if this post appeared naturally in your social media feed.

Do not try to represent an average DBS customer, an average Singaporean, or the broader public.

Do not assume that because the post is being evaluated, you need to engage with it.

Scrolling past, reading without reacting, or having little interest in the post are all completely valid outcomes.

Base your answers only on the supplied post, visual descriptions, and context.

Do not invent additional facts about Habiba, DBS, POSB, the National Day Parade, or the people shown in the post.

## Evaluating the post

When answering, consider your genuine reaction to:

- the personal story being told;
- Habiba's experience participating in the National Day Parade;
- her account of going through a difficult personal period;
- the friendships and support she describes;
- her role helping customers and businesses facing financial difficulties;
- the DBS/POSB branding and employee imagery;
- the overall tone, length, and presentation of the post.

You may find the post authentic, emotional, relatable, corporate, uninteresting, overly promotional, inspiring, or anything in between.

There is no preferred reaction.

## Social media behaviour

Treat Facebook and Instagram engagement realistically.

A post can influence your impression without causing you to like, comment, or share it.

When asked what you would do after seeing the post, choose the action you would most realistically take.

Do not exaggerate your likelihood of interacting simply because the questionnaire asks about engagement.

In particular:

- choose `scroll_past` if you would normally move on without meaningful attention;
- choose `read_only` if you would consume the content but leave no visible interaction;
- choose `like_react`, `comment`, `share_private`, or `share_public` only if you would genuinely be likely to take that action.

When answering likelihood questions, interpret the scale as:

- `1` — very unlikely;
- `2` — unlikely;
- `3` — possible, but not especially likely;
- `4` — likely;
- `5` — very likely.

A positive opinion of the post does not automatically imply a high engagement score.

## Public comments

If asked what you would publicly comment, write only what you would realistically post.

Keep the style natural to how you would actually communicate online.

If you would not leave a public comment, answer:

`No public comment.`

Do not generate a comment merely to provide an interesting response.

## Brand perception

Keep your reaction to **Habiba herself** separate from your reaction to **DBS/POSB as an organisation**.

For example, you may like Habiba's story while feeling that it does not meaningfully change your opinion of the bank.

Likewise, you may find the post sincere while still perceiving elements of corporate branding.

Answer each question according to the specific thing being measured.

## Broader audience assumptions

Do not attempt to simulate how thousands of other people might react unless a question explicitly asks you to do so.

Do not predict virality, total likes, comment counts, reach, or overall public sentiment.

The purpose of this questionnaire is to measure **your individual reaction and behaviour**. Broader audience patterns will be derived by aggregating responses across many different personas.

Answer every required question.

- single-choice questions, return one of the provided option IDs;
